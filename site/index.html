How we define hate

We measure hostility toward Indians as people: people of Indian origin, including the diaspora. Criticism of the Indian government, its policies, its army, its cricket team or its films is not counted.

LevelNameExample patternWeight
0Neutral / critiqueNews, questions, praise, policy criticism0.00
1Negative stereotyping“Indians are all scammers”0.25
2Contempt / slursDisgust, insults, slurs0.50
3DehumanisationAnimals, vermin, disease, filth0.75
4Exclusion / violenceDeport, ban, expel, attack1.00

What gets thrown out

  • Sarcasm is excluded entirely, from both the count of hateful posts and the total. Signals: /s, “yeah right”, “sure, because…”, “said no one ever”, irony emoji (🙄 🙃 😏 🤡) on otherwise positive wording, and scare-quoted stereotypes. The share removed is shown for every country.
  • Counter-speech (“it’s racist to say Indians smell”) and reporting about racism count as non-hateful.
  • Negations (“not all Indians…”, “Indians aren’t scammers”) don’t count as endorsing the stereotype.

From posts to a country score

  1. Each post gets the weight of the highest level it reaches.
  2. Each person counts once. We average each author’s posts first, so one account spamming 500 slurs counts as one person.
  3. Country score = mean author score × 100.
  4. Small samples are pulled toward the global average: (n·raw + 30·global) / (n + 30).
  5. Confidence: low (< 50 authors), medium (< 300), high (≥ 300).

Why people come to hate a racial group

These drivers recur in research on prejudice (Allport, integrated threat theory, realistic conflict theory, social identity theory). The index’s “reasons” come from them.

Economic competitionJobs, wages, housing, services. Peaks in downturns.
Cultural / symbolic threatFood, religion, language framed as a threat to “our way of life”.
Demographic anxiety“Too many of them”, “invasion”, “replacement”.
In-group favouritismOut-groups judged by their worst members.
Low or skewed contactViews formed from memes and news, not people.
ScapegoatingA visible minority blamed for broad problems.
Meme & media amplificationOne viral clip becomes “what they’re all like”.
Political rhetoricHostility normalised for votes or engagement.
Historical / geopolitical conflictRivalries spill onto ordinary people.
Status & hierarchyColourism, class contempt, resentment of success.
Disgust-based prejudiceHygiene and smell tropes: viral and dehumanising.
Fear of crime & fraudSome members’ crimes generalised to everyone.

The common feeling toward Indians online

Indians are one of the largest and most visible migrant groups online, with a diaspora of about 35 million and a huge domestic internet population. Reporting and research since 2023 point to a consistent pattern:

  • A sharp rise in anti-Indian content on X, Reddit and 4chan, including slurs such as “pajeet” and recycled meme formats. Spikes follow H-1B debates in the US and immigration and student-visa debates in Canada, the UK and Australia.
  • Jobs and immigration are the dominant frame in English-speaking countries: H-1B and “Indians hiring Indians” in the US, international students and housing in Canada, overall migration levels in the UK and Australia.
  • Disgust memes about street defecation, smell and street food are the most viral strand and the most dehumanising.
  • Scam stereotypes are fuelled by real call-centre fraud, then generalised to all Indians.
  • Gendered tropes: Indian men as sexually aggressive (“bobs and vegana” spam).
  • Regional rivalry with Pakistan and Bangladesh, and resentment of Indian influence in some neighbouring countries.
  • The Gulf shows a different pattern: class contempt toward low-wage workers rather than “replacement” rhetoric.
  • Counter-currents: most conversation about Indians is neutral or positive (tech leaders, food, cricket, film). A lot of the hostility comes from a small number of very active accounts, which is why we count people, not posts.

Common stereotypes, and the reality

The classifier tags each of these. They are listed so they can be recognised and measured, not because they’re true.

StereotypeReality
Indians smell / smell of currySmell slurs have targeted almost every migrant group (Italians, Irish, Chinese). Cooking aromas aren’t body odour.
Indians are dirty / defecate in the streetRural open defecation was an infrastructure problem and fell sharply after 2014. It says nothing about migrants abroad.
Indians are scammersOrganised call-centre fraud is prosecuted in India too, and Indians are among its victims. It’s the work of a tiny criminal fraction.
Indians take our jobs / abuse H-1BHost governments design and cap visa programmes. Holders are legally hired, mostly in shortage fields.
Indians only hire other IndiansNetwork hiring happens in every community. Real discrimination is individual misconduct covered by law.
Indians are overrunning / invadingReplacement rhetoric. Indians are a single-digit share of the population in every Western country.
Indian men are creepsDriven by viral spam and a handful of cases. Harassment exists in every society.
Indians are cheap / stingyThe same trope has been aimed at Scots, Jews and Chinese people. Often it’s just high savings rates.
Indians are poor / third worldIndia is a top-five economy, and the diaspora is among the highest-earning groups in several countries.
Indians worship cows / are superstitiousMockery of religious practice. Every faith looks strange from outside.
Indians are all tech support / ITThe diaspora spans medicine, law, business, trades and the arts.
Indians are loud / rudeNorms vary by culture and person. The same trope is applied to many groups.
Indians are bad driversA generic immigrant stereotype with no group-level evidence.
Indian degrees / doctors are fakeIndian-trained professionals pass the same host-country licensing exams.
Regional / geopolitical hostilityDisputes between states get turned into contempt for ordinary people.

Sources & limits

Only public data that needs no login or API key. Collection runs every hour on GitHub Actions, and usernames are hashed before storage.
